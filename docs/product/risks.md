# Overtone — Risk Register

Scale: likelihood / impact each **L=1, M=2, H=3**; score = L×I. Review at every increment exit. A risk whose score rises twice running gets scope taken off the roadmap, not a stronger mitigation sentence.

**The register shrank.** The free hosted stack (Groq + Vercel Hobby + Supabase, no card anywhere) retired every cost, vendor-procurement and legal-gate risk this register used to carry. Those rows are kept below, marked **RETIRED**, with the reason — so a future reversal has to argue with the reason rather than rediscover the risk. What replaced them is mundane, arithmetical and real: two-hour episodes chunked and stitched back together, an hourly audio-second allowance, a database that falls asleep, and no speaker diarisation at all.

Not legal advice. It also no longer needs to be: the group records itself, there are no third-party subjects, no public signup and no money.

## Live risks

| ID | Risk | Category | L | I | Score | Early warning | Mitigation | Where |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **R22** | **Timestamp stitching across chunks — the highest-risk code in the project.** A 2-hour episode is split into ~5 chunks; each chunk's word timestamps start at zero and must be offset by that chunk's true start. An off-by-one in one offset silently points *every subsequent count* at the wrong moment in the audio. The transcript still reads fine, the counts are still the right totals, and every quote link is wrong | technical | H | H | 9 | A quote that doesn't match the audio at its timestamp; a seam that loses or duplicates a sentence; density strips with a suspicious step at a 25-minute mark | Offsets computed from the chunk plan, never inferred from the response; split on silence so boundaries are findable; a committed multi-chunk fixture with hand-checked word positions, asserted at **every** seam (±1 s); a test that deliberately corrupts one offset and must fail; M2's "every quote contains the matched form at the right timestamp" check is the standing regression on this | M1 (built) · M2 (regression) |
| **R1** | **Transcription mangles the in-joke words.** Counts inherit every ASR error, and the words that matter most to a podcast group are exactly the ones Whisper gets wrong: coinages, nicknames, deliberate nonsense, proper nouns | technical | H | H | 9 | Keyword recall <90% on named bits; the group says "that's not what we said"; quote drills show quotes missing the bit | Golden set of 2 real two-hour episodes from week one; measure *recall on named bits*, not WER; every count drills to a verifiable quote so the group audits it themselves; per-episode confidence flag; synonym sets absorb the misspellings the ASR consistently produces | M0 baseline · M2 bar |
| **R2** | **Variant gaps silently undercount.** "hotdog" / "hot dog" / "hot-dogs" / "Hotdogs", plus whatever the ASR invents. Now a central product risk, because a running joke *is* a term with variants | technical / product | H | H | 9 | Accuracy fails on specific bits while the transcript reads fine; the group adds the same bit twice with different spellings | A bit = name + synonym set (ADR 0006); case/plural/hyphen/whitespace folding with a unit test per class; **show the matched surface forms next to every count** so a gap is visible, not silent; one-click "add this variant" from the quote list | M2 |
| **R21** | **Supabase pauses after 7 idle days.** A weekly or fortnightly show will leave gaps, and a friend will open the app to a broken page — precisely the failure of the "works without my computer running" promise | technical | H | H | 9 | ≥5 days with no DB activity; a cold-start error on first load after a gap | Daily keep-alive cron hitting a trivial query (Vercel Hobby's daily cron is enough); M1 exit criterion is surviving an 8-day gap; if it ever still pauses, the page says "waking up" rather than throwing | M1 |
| **R20** | **The hourly audio-second wall.** Free tier is **7,200 audio-seconds/hour and 28,800/day**. A 2-hour episode *is* 7,200 seconds, so one episode spends the whole hour: **one 2-hour episode per hour, four per day, shared by everybody.** Invisible for a weekly show; immediate the first time two friends upload after a recording session | technical / product | H | M | 6 | Any 429 from Groq; a second upload failing within the hour; >4 h of audio used in a day | Treat the limit as a **product state, not an error**: "this hour's free transcription is used up, try again in ~N minutes", shown before the upload starts where possible; parallel chunks cost nothing extra (duration is what's spent, and 5 requests is far under 20/min); auth from M1 so strangers can't spend the allowance; **no test ever calls Groq** (`CLAUDE.md`); daily headroom on the health check | M1 |
| **R26** | **The back catalogue cannot be imported quickly.** 50 existing episodes × 2 h = 100 audio-hours; at four episodes a day that is **about two weeks of drip-feeding**. The series view is the whole point, and it is thin until several episodes exist | product | H | M | 6 | The group asks to "just upload them all"; an import attempt dying on 429 halfway | Say the number out loud before anyone tries: two weeks, not an afternoon. Start the series with the next few *new* episodes rather than the archive. Nothing is built for this unless they ask; if they do, it is an honest "12 of 50 imported, next at 14:30" screen and no cleverness — the wall is the free tier, not the code | *Later* lane |
| **R24** | **No speaker diarisation exists in the free stack**, and the obvious workaround is wrong. "Who talks most" was explicitly wanted. Transcribing one track per host double-counts (every mic picks up the other hosts faintly) *and* costs 3 × 2 h = 6 audio-hours against a 2-hour hourly allowance | product / technical | H | M | 6 | The group asks for talk time; anyone proposes transcribing tracks separately, or estimating speakers from a mixed file | **Energy attribution, one transcription:** transcribe the mixed file once, then use the per-host tracks only as loudness signals and attribute each word to whichever track is loudest at that timestamp. Exact, free, no duplicates. Not scheduled; gated on the group confirming they already record per-host tracks (Q13). A mixed-only file gets no speaker breakdown and the UI says why. Never ship an estimate | *Later* lane |
| **R16** | **The analytics might just not be interesting.** Counts could all be correct and change nothing about the show | product | M | H | 6 | Series view opened less than once per episode; fewer than 5 bits defined; nobody can name something it told them | M2's bar is the group naming ≥5 real bits themselves, M3's is them pointing at one thing they didn't know; hand-make the series table in a spreadsheet for 3 past episodes *before* building M3's UI; watchlists over auto-discovered top terms, because the group's own bits are the only ones they care about | M2/M3 validation |
| **R14** | **The chatbot gets pulled forward.** Most demoable, least load-bearing, and tempting precisely because it's fun | product | H | M | 6 | Anybody starts on the assistant before M3 ships | Sequenced into *Later*, behind the one `assistant` flag; written precondition — no assistant work until the series view ships and counts are trusted, since the assistant is only as right as the counts it reads; counting stays in SQL (ADR 0007) | *Later* |
| **R23** | **Both free tiers fill at a comparable rate.** A 2-hour episode is ~21 MB of compressed audio and ~18–20k words, so roughly 2–5 MB of transcript plus word-level timestamps. 1 GB of file storage is **~37 episodes** if audio is kept; 500 MB of Postgres is a few hundred episodes of transcript. A weekly show reaches the audio ceiling inside a year | technical | M | M | 4 | File storage >500 MB; DB >250 MB; an upload failing on quota | **Resolved by ADR 0013 — keep recent audio, auto-purge oldest-first** once total audio passes ~80% of the tier. The library grows without limit; only *playback* has a horizon, and a purged episode keeps every count, quote and timestamp. Episodes can be pinned to exempt them. The purge reuses the keep-alive cron that already exists for R21. Per-episode delete removes its blob too, asserted by test | M1 |
| **R18** | **Input audio quality dominates error** — one shared room mic, crosstalk, a laptop fan. | **R25** | **Vercel Hobby is non-commercial.** If this ever takes money — donations, a Patreon tier, an ad read — the hosting terms are breached and the stack stops being free | product | L | M | 2 | Any conversation about charging, sponsoring or accepting donations | Keep it a hobby app (Q12 is settled: free). ## Retired risks

Kept with their ids so the history is traceable. Each needs a stated reason to come back.

| ID | Was | Status | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| R3 | Transcription cost, then local-inference throughput | **RETIRED** | Groq is free and runs ~160× realtime. There is no invoice and no slow local model. The residual speed concern is R6; the residual *allowance* concern is R20. |
| R4 | LLM cost explodes on library-wide questions | **RETIRED** | Groq's free LLMs, and the assistant is an unscheduled *Later* lane. The aggregates-first design (ADR 0007) survives as a **correctness** rule — never let a model count — not as a cost control. |
| R5 | Unmetered endpoint burns the ASR budget | **RETIRED as a cost risk** | No budget to burn. The residual — a stranger spending the group's hourly allowance — is R20, mitigated by magic-link auth from M1. |
| R7 | Non-idempotent retries cause double ASR charges | **RETIRED** | No charges. Re-uploading an episode now costs an hour of allowance (R20) and a delete button's worth of tidying, not money. |
| R9 | Consent / all-party recording law | **RETIRED** | The group records itself and uploads its own show. No covert capture, no third-party subjects, no public signup. No legal review is being scheduled, and that is a deliberate decision rather than an oversight. |
| R10 | PII reaching a subprocessor; DPA, retention and region review | **RETIRED as a procurement gate** | Nothing to procure and no DPA to negotiate on a free tier. Honest residual, handled as **one sentence of UI copy** at M4: episode audio is sent to Groq for transcription, and the group should know that before uploading. No vendor review, no legal gate. |
| R11 | No working deletion path; GDPR-style erasure | **RETIRED as a compliance risk** | Folded into R23 as a **capacity** concern: delete must free the blob because 1 GB is the ceiling. Per-episode delete still ships at M1 and is still tested. |
| R12 | Diarisation unreliable (DER 10–20%) | **RETIRED** | There is no diarisation to be unreliable — the free stack has none. Replaced by R24: energy attribution from per-host tracks, or no speaker breakdown at all. |
| R15 | "Nice UI" is unbounded | **RETIRED as a register-level risk** | M4 is one hard-timeboxed weekend and the stakeholder is a user. The old 10-item checklist and two reference products were ceremony for a product being sold. Unfinished polish gets cut, not carried. |
| R8 | Large uploads fail — browser memory, proxy limits, 200 MB over hotel wifi | **RETIRED as framed** | Compressed chunks are ~4–5 MB each and upload independently, so a failure costs one chunk, not an episode. What remains of it is R22 (stitching) and R6 (wall clock). |
| — | Budget ceiling, spend alerts, kill switches, cost-per-audio-hour, cost per active user | **RETIRED** | No money is in this system at any point. See the anti-metrics in [`metrics.md`](./metrics.md). |

## Cheapest experiment per live risk

All free, almost all under two hours. "Kill criterion" = the observation that forces a plan change.

| ID | Experiment | When | Kill criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| R22 | Take one real two-hour episode. Split it on silence into 5 chunks in the browser, compress to 16 kHz mono Opus, check each is well under 25 MB, transcribe two adjacent chunks, and verify by ear that a word 50 minutes in lands at the timestamp the transcript claims. | **Before M1 code** | Offsets drift, or a seam loses words ⇒ the chunk plan is the design problem to solve first, and M1 ships checkpoint (a) before anything else |
| R20 | Upload two two-hour episodes back to back and watch exactly what Groq returns on the second. | **Before M1 code** | The 429 surfaces as an unreadable error ⇒ the rate-limit message is M1 scope, not polish |
| R1 | Transcribe 2 real episodes. Hand-count 10 named bits. Compute recall per bit. | **Before M2 code** | Recall <85% on the group's own in-joke words ⇒ seed synonym sets from the ASR's actual misspellings before building the UI |
| R2 | In that same sheet, list every surface form Groq produced for each of the 10 bits. | with R1 | >3 unmatched variants per bit ⇒ one-click "add this variant" is M2 scope, not later |
| R21 | Leave the Supabase project alone for 8 days, then load the app. | once, now | It pauses ⇒ the keep-alive cron is M1's first commit |
| R26 | Count the episodes in the archive and divide by four. Tell the group the number of days. | **now**, one message | They expect the archive on day one ⇒ reset that expectation before building anything, because no code shortens it |
| R24 | Ask the group one question: do you already record one file per host? | **now** (Q13) | No ⇒ "who talks most" never ships. Say so plainly and stop discussing it |
| R16 | Hand-build the series table for 3 past episodes in a spreadsheet. Show the group. Ask what they'd look at next. | **Before M3 UI** | Nobody reacts ⇒ the series view is the wrong shape; find out what they'd actually compare before building it |
| R14 | Nothing to run. The precondition is written here and in the roadmap. | now | Assistant work starts before M3 ships ⇒ stop and re-read |
| R23 | Upload an episode, confirm success, then look in the storage bucket; note the DB size for one episode. | M1 | Audio is still there ⇒ deletion-on-success is a bug, fix before the next episode |
| R18 | From R1's data, compare recall between episodes. | with R1 | >2× spread ⇒ the per-episode confidence flag is v1 scope |
| R6 | Time the slowest real episode end to end, in prod, including browser compression. | M1 exit | >120 s ⇒ smaller chunks or harder compression, both one config value |
| R13 | One non-English clip through Groq with detection off. | before M4 | Confident English nonsense ⇒ the warning banner is M4 scope |
| R17 | Keep the Groq call behind the port with a fake, from commit one. | M0 | Provider details leak into counting logic ⇒ refactor before M1 |
| R19 | After each weekend, ask: is what's on `main` usable by a friend right now? | weekly | Two weekends running with nothing shippable ⇒ the slices are too big, cut them |

**Sequencing note.** R22, R20, R26 and R24 are the four that can change the plan, and together they cost one evening on a real episode plus two messages to the group. Do all four before M1 starts.
