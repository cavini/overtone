# Overtone — Risk Register

Scale: likelihood / impact each **L=1, M=2, H=3**; score = L×I. Review at every increment exit. A risk whose score rises twice running gets scope taken off the roadmap, not a stronger mitigation sentence.

**The register shrank, then changed shape.** The free hosted stack (Groq + Vercel Hobby + Supabase, no card anywhere) retired every cost, vendor-procurement and legal-gate risk this register used to carry; the speaker-attribution row went with the news that there are no per-host tracks. Retired rows are kept below with their ids and a stated reason, so a future reversal has to argue with the reason rather than rediscover the risk.

**This round it moved in both directions at once.** Two stakeholder answers landed. **The interface must be bilingual** — pt-BR and English, switchable (ADR 0017) — which adds **R30** (locale number formatting) and **R31** (the UI locale leaking into the counting path). **There is no back catalogue** — only new episodes (Q16) — which **retires R26** outright, downgrades **R23** from 4 to 2 for want of an import burst, and leaves behind one honest inheritance: **R32**, the series view shipping into an empty library. Net on the register: **+3 live rows, −1 retired, one downgrade.** Net on the roadmap: +2 weekends. The reduction and the addition are not in the same currency and should not be netted off against each other.

**What dominates it now is the language.** The show is recorded in **Brazilian Portuguese**, and four of the five worst rows here are consequences of that: pt-BR word error rates are higher than English and worst on exactly the words this product counts (R1); Whisper prefers written-standard Portuguese, so a count can move because of a spelling the speaker never used (R27); a Portuguese stemmer over-merges genuinely different words (R28); and stripping diacritics merges distinct words outright (R29). All four produce a **wrong number that reads as a right one** — the failure this product exists to avoid. The remaining severe row is the old one, timestamp stitching (R22).

Not legal advice. It also no longer needs to be: the group records itself, there are no third-party subjects, no public signup and no money.

**Ranking:** R1, R27, R22, R28, R2, R21 (9) → R29, R31, R30, R20, R32, R16, R14 (6) → R18, R19, R13 (4) → R23, R25, R6, R17 (2).

The scale tops out at 9, so it cannot separate the six rows that sit there. **R1 leads them on judgement, not arithmetic:** the other five are things we can engineer around once we know they exist, while R1 is the one that can make this product impossible for this audio. It is settled by one evening of work — see the first row of the experiments table.

**The 6-band is now crowded and should not be read as one kind of thing.** Three of its rows — **R29, R30, R31** — are *deterministic*: they have a known wrong default, a one-line correct answer, and a test that pins it. They sit at 6 only until M0 ships, after which they are CI furniture and should be re-scored down. **R32 is not uncertainty at all, it is arithmetic**: the series will be empty, and the only question is whether anybody is surprised by it. The rows that deserve attention at 6 are the two that depend on people — **R16** (nobody cares) and **R14** (the assistant gets pulled forward).

**Top four to carry into M0, by judgement:** **R1** (pt-BR recall — can end the project), **R22** (timestamp stitching — the highest-risk code), **R27/R28** jointly (Whisper's orthography and stemmer over-merge — the two ways a count moves without anyone noticing), and **R32** (the product's headline value is unavailable for its first month, and that is now certain rather than possible).
