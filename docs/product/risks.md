# Overtone — Risk Register

Scale: likelihood / impact each **L=1, M=2, H=3**; score = L×I. Review at every increment exit. A risk whose score rises twice running gets scope taken off the roadmap, not a stronger mitigation sentence.

**The register shrank, then changed shape.** The free hosted stack (Groq + Vercel Hobby + Supabase, no card anywhere) retired every cost, vendor-procurement and legal-gate risk this register used to carry; the speaker-attribution row went with the news that there are no per-host tracks. Retired rows are kept below with their ids and a stated reason, so a future reversal has to argue with the reason rather than rediscover the risk.

**What dominates it now is the language.** The show is recorded in **Brazilian Portuguese**, and four of the five worst rows here are consequences of that: pt-BR word error rates are higher than English and worst on exactly the words this product counts (R1); Whisper prefers written-standard Portuguese, so a count can move because of a spelling the speaker never used (R27); a Portuguese stemmer over-merges genuinely different words (R28); and stripping diacritics merges distinct words outright (R29). All four produce a **wrong number that reads as a right one** — the failure this product exists to avoid. The remaining severe row is the old one, timestamp stitching (R22).

Not legal advice. It also no longer needs to be: the group records itself, there are no third-party subjects, no public signup and no money.

| **R1** | **pt-BR transcription mangles the in-joke words — the most severe row on this list.** Counts inherit every ASR error; Portuguese word error rates are higher than English; and the worst cases are proper nouns, slang and coinages. The in-jokes *are* the product, so this risk does not degrade Overtone, it decides whether Overtone works at all for this audio | technical | H | H | 9 | Recall <90% on named bits in the ten-minute pilot; the group says "that's not what we said"; nicknames coming back as ordinary Portuguese words; quote drills showing quotes that don't contain the bit | **Run the ten-minute pilot before anything else is built** — first row of the experiments table, one evening, free, and it can end the project cheaply. `language=pt` pinned so the model never guesses (ADR 0014). | **R27** | **Whisper emits written-standard Portuguese.** Casual spoken forms arrive silently rewritten as their formal equivalents — `pra` → `para`, `tá` → `está`, `né` → `não é`, `vamo` → `vamos`. Show the matched surface forms next to the count so the substitution is visible. Never rewrite the stored transcript — normalise at match time only | M2 (CI-permanent) |
| **R22** | **Timestamp stitching across chunks — the highest-risk code in the project.** A 2-hour episode is split into ~5 chunks; each chunk's word timestamps start at zero and must be offset by that chunk's true start. An off-by-one in one offset silently points *every subsequent count* at the wrong moment in the audio. The transcript still reads fine, the counts are still the right totals, and every quote link is wrong | technical | H | H | 9 | A quote that doesn't match the audio at its timestamp; a seam that loses or duplicates a sentence; density strips with a suspicious step at a 25-minute mark | Offsets computed from the chunk plan, never inferred from the response; split on silence so boundaries are findable; a committed multi-chunk fixture with hand-checked word positions, asserted at **every** seam (±1 s); a test that deliberately corrupts one offset and must fail; M2's "every quote contains the matched form at the right timestamp" check is the standing regression on this | M1 (built) · M2 (regression) |
| **R28** | **Over-merging from Portuguese stemming.** Aggressive stemmers collapse genuinely different words — `casa`/`casar`, and `comer` colliding with the preposition `com`. A false merge **inflates a count invisibly**: the number rises, the quote list looks plausible, nothing errors. A lemma dictionary does not save us on its own, because the words that fall through to the stemmer are the out-of-vocabulary ones — which is precisely what a coinage or a nickname is | technical | H | H | 9 | A count rising with no quotes a human would accept; a bit matching a function word; a stem under ~3 characters matching everything | Lemma **dictionary first, stemmer only as fallback, and no merge the dictionary contradicts** (ADR 0014); a committed table of pt-BR near-miss pairs (`casa`/`casar`, `com`/`comer`, `vez`/`ver`) asserted distinct in CI; a minimum stem length and a function-word blocklist so no bit can match a preposition; matched surface forms next to every count, so an over-merge shows up as a quote nobody accepts rather than as a silent +3 | M2 |
| **R2** | **Variant gaps silently undercount.** "hotdog" / "hot dog" / "hot-dogs" / "Hotdogs", plus whatever the ASR invents — and in Portuguese also gender and number agreement (`o cachorro-quente` / `os cachorros-quentes`) and verb conjugation (`comer` / `comi` / `comeram`). Now a central product risk, because a running joke *is* a term with variants | technical / product | H | H | 9 | Accuracy fails on specific bits while the transcript reads fine; the group adds the same bit twice with different spellings | A bit = name + synonym set (ADR 0006); case/plural/hyphen/whitespace folding with a unit test per class, plus agreement and conjugation via the lemma dictionary; **show the matched surface forms next to every count** so a gap is visible, not silent; one-click "add this variant" from the quote list | M2 |
| **R21** | **Supabase pauses after 7 idle days.** A weekly or fortnightly show will leave gaps, and a friend will open the app to a broken page — precisely the failure of the "works without my computer running" promise | technical | H | H | 9 | ≥5 days with no DB activity; a cold-start error on first load after a gap | Daily keep-alive cron hitting a trivial query (Vercel Hobby's daily cron is enough); M1 exit criterion is surviving an 8-day gap; if it ever still pauses, the page says "waking up" rather than throwing | M1 |
| **R29** | **Diacritics must be preserved.** The reflex "just normalise the text" snippet strips accents, and in Portuguese that merges distinct words: `e`/`é` (and / is), `por`/`pôr` (for / to put), `esta`/`está`, `secretaria`/`secretária`. One line of normalisation corrupts every count downstream, and nothing anywhere reports an error | technical | M | H | 6 | `é` showing up folded into the stop-word-filtered `e`; a two-letter term with an absurd count; any normalisation helper that decomposes to NFD and strips combining marks | **Casefold only — never accent-strip, anywhere in the counting path** (ADR 0014). NFC once at ingest and nowhere else. A diacritics test is in **M0's** exit criteria pinning `e`/`é` and `por`/`pôr` as distinct tokens, and it stays in CI forever. The normalisation version string is already stored on every Analysis, so a regression is traceable to exactly the episodes it touched | **M0** · CI forever |
| **R20** | **The hourly audio-second wall.** Free tier is **7,200 audio-seconds/hour and 28,800/day**. A 2-hour episode *is* 7,200 seconds, so one episode spends the whole hour: **one 2-hour episode per hour, four per day, shared by everybody.** Invisible for a weekly show; immediate the first time two friends upload after a recording session | technical / product | H | M | 6 | Any 429 from Groq; a second upload failing within the hour; >4 h of audio used in a day | Treat the limit as a **product state, not an error**: "this hour's free transcription is used up, try again in ~N minutes", shown before the upload starts where possible; parallel chunks cost nothing extra (duration is what's spent, and 5 requests is far under 20/min); auth from M1 so strangers can't spend the allowance; **no test ever calls Groq** (`CLAUDE.md`); daily headroom on the health check | M1 |
| **R26** | **The back catalogue cannot be imported quickly.** 50 existing episodes × 2 h = 100 audio-hours; at four episodes a day that is **about two weeks of drip-feeding**. The series view is the whole point, and it is thin until several episodes exist | product | H | M | 6 | The group asks to "just upload them all"; an import attempt dying on 429 halfway | Say the number out loud before anyone tries: two weeks, not an afternoon. ## Retired risks

Kept with their ids so the history is traceable. Each needs a stated reason to come back.

| ID | Was | Status | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| R3 | Transcription cost, then local-inference throughput | **RETIRED** | Groq is free and runs ~160× realtime. There is no invoice and no slow local model. The residual speed concern is R6; the residual *allowance* concern is R20. |
| R4 | LLM cost explodes on library-wide questions | **RETIRED** | Groq's free LLMs, and the assistant is an unscheduled *Later* lane. The aggregates-first design (ADR 0007) survives as a **correctness** rule — never let a model count — not as a cost control. |
| R5 | Unmetered endpoint burns the ASR budget | **RETIRED as a cost risk** | No budget to burn. The residual — a stranger spending the group's hourly allowance — is R20, mitigated by magic-link auth from M1. |
| R7 | Non-idempotent retries cause double ASR charges | **RETIRED** | No charges. Re-uploading an episode now costs an hour of allowance (R20) and a delete button's worth of tidying, not money. |
| R9 | Consent / all-party recording law | **RETIRED** | The group records itself and uploads its own show. No covert capture, no third-party subjects, no public signup. No legal review is being scheduled, and that is a deliberate decision rather than an oversight. |
| R10 | PII reaching a subprocessor; DPA, retention and region review | **RETIRED as a procurement gate** | Nothing to procure and no DPA to negotiate on a free tier. Honest residual, handled as **one sentence of UI copy** at M4: episode audio is sent to Groq for transcription, and the group should know that before uploading. No vendor review, no legal gate. |
| R11 | No working deletion path; GDPR-style erasure | **RETIRED as a compliance risk** | Folded into R23 as a **capacity** concern: delete must free the blob because 1 GB is the ceiling. Per-episode delete still ships at M1 and is still tested. |
| R24 | No speaker diarisation in the free stack; "who talks most" via per-host track energy | **RETIRED** | **There are no per-host tracks.** The stakeholder: *"they would only upload the final edited file."* Energy attribution needed one track per host as a loudness signal, so the workaround has no inputs and the feature is **cut from the product, not deferred** (ADR 0015). Q13 is moot. Nothing is gated on it and there is no *Later* lane for it. To come back, the group would have to change how they record **and** start uploading multi-track exports — a different product conversation, not a backlog item. |
| R12 | Diarisation unreliable (DER 10–20%) | **RETIRED** | There is no diarisation to be unreliable — the free stack has none, and the energy workaround that replaced this row is itself retired (R24). There is no speaker dimension in the product at all. |
| R15 | "Nice UI" is unbounded | **RETIRED as a register-level risk** | M4 is one hard-timeboxed weekend and the stakeholder is a user. The old 10-item checklist and two reference products were ceremony for a product being sold. Unfinished polish gets cut, not carried. |
| R8 | Large uploads fail — browser memory, proxy limits, 200 MB over hotel wifi | **RETIRED as framed** | Compressed chunks are ~4–5 MB each and upload independently, so a failure costs one chunk, not an episode. What remains of it is R22 (stitching) and R6 (wall clock). |

## Cheapest experiment per live risk

All free, almost all under two hours. "Kill criterion" = the observation that forces a plan change.

| ID | Experiment | When | Kill criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| **R1** | **The pt-BR recall pilot — do this first, before anything else in this table.** Take ten minutes of one real episode. Send it through the actual Groq service with `language=pt`. Write down a list of in-joke terms and proper nouns you *know* are in those ten minutes, then hand-check the transcript for each one and record **recall**. One evening, free, no code. It is the only experiment here that can tell you the product does not work for this audio. | **before M0** | Recall <85% on the group's own in-joke words and names ⇒ stop and decide what Overtone is instead: seed synonym sets from the ASR's actual output, lean on a prompt-vocabulary hint, or accept that counting coinages is not possible and the product is about recurring *topics* rather than *bits*. Do not start M0 on the assumption it will be fine |
| **R27** | In the same ten minutes, list every spot where the transcript is more formal than the speech: `pra`→`para`, `tá`→`está`, `né`→`não é`. Count how many of the hand-checked terms were affected. | with R1 | Any in-joke term is affected ⇒ the colloquial↔standard alias table and its equal-counts test are M2 scope on day one, not a follow-up |
| **R28** | Take the 50 most frequent words from that transcript, run them through the candidate Portuguese stemmer, and read the groups it produces. Look specifically for `com`/`comer` and `casa`/`casar`. | with R1, before M2 | The stemmer merges any pair a human would call different ⇒ the dictionary is authoritative, the stemmer never overrides it, and the near-miss table is written before the matcher |
| **R29** | One unit test, five minutes: assert `e`/`é` and `por`/`pôr` tokenise as distinct terms. Write it in M0. | **M0, first commit** | It passes only after accent-stripping is removed ⇒ you already had the bug; keep the test forever |
| R22 | Take one real two-hour episode. Split it on silence into 5 chunks in the browser, compress to 16 kHz mono Opus, check each is well under 25 MB, transcribe two adjacent chunks, and verify by ear that a word 50 minutes in lands at the timestamp the transcript claims. | **Before M1 code** | Offsets drift, or a seam loses words ⇒ the chunk plan is the design problem to solve first, and M1 ships checkpoint (a) before anything else |
| R20 | Upload two two-hour episodes back to back and watch exactly what Groq returns on the second. | **Before M1 code** | The 429 surfaces as an unreadable error ⇒ the rate-limit message is M1 scope, not polish |
| R1 (scaled) | The pilot at full size: 2 real episodes, 10 named bits, recall per bit. This is the golden set M2 is measured against, not a repeat of the pilot. | **Before M2 code** | Recall materially worse than the ten-minute pilot suggested ⇒ the pilot sample was lucky; re-forecast M2 before building the UI |
| R2 | In that same sheet, list every surface form Groq produced for each of the 10 bits, pt-BR agreement and conjugation forms included. | with R1 | >3 unmatched variants per bit ⇒ one-click "add this variant" is M2 scope, not later |
| R21 | Leave the Supabase project alone for 8 days, then load the app. | once, now | It pauses ⇒ the keep-alive cron is M1's first commit |
| R26 | Count the episodes in the archive and divide by four. Tell the group the number of days. | **now**, one message | They expect the archive on day one ⇒ reset that expectation before building anything, because no code shortens it |
| R16 | Hand-build the series table for 3 past episodes in a spreadsheet. Show the group. Ask what they'd look at next. | **Before M3 UI** | Nobody reacts ⇒ the series view is the wrong shape; find out what they'd actually compare before building it |
| R14 | Nothing to run. The precondition is written here and in the roadmap. | now | Assistant work starts before M3 ships ⇒ stop and re-read |
| R23 | Upload an episode, confirm success, then look in the storage bucket; note the DB size for one episode. | M1 | Audio is still there ⇒ deletion-on-success is a bug, fix before the next episode |
| R18 | From R1's data, compare recall between episodes. | with R1 | >2× spread ⇒ the per-episode confidence flag is v1 scope |
| R6 | Time the slowest real episode end to end, in prod, including browser compression. | M1 exit | >120 s ⇒ smaller chunks or harder compression, both one config value |
| R13 | Put two code-switched English terms the group actually uses on the pilot's hand-check list, and write down the spellings Groq returns for them. | with R1 | Each spelled a different way ⇒ English loanwords need aliases seeded from data, and the bit-naming UI must say so |
| R17 | Keep the Groq call behind the port with a fake, from commit one. | M0 | Provider details leak into counting logic ⇒ refactor before M1 |
| R19 | After each weekend, ask: is what's on `main` usable by a friend right now? | weekly | Two weekends running with nothing shippable ⇒ the slices are too big, cut them |

After that, R22, R20 and R26 are the three that can change the plan, and together they cost one evening on a real episode plus one message to the group (Q16). Do them before M1 starts. R24's experiment is gone: there are no per-host tracks to ask about.
